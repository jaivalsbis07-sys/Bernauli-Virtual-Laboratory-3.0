Fundamentals

Theoretical Background

Basic Concept

"According to Bernoulli's theorem, for an ideal, steady, incompressible and irrotational fluid, the sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy and potential energy is constant along a streamline."
Total Energy Equation:
pγ + v²2g + Z = Constant (Total Head)

When Bernoulli's equation applies

  • Steady flow: Velocity and pressure at a point do not change with time.
  • Incompressible fluid: Constant density, which is an accurate assumption for water.
  • No friction (inviscid): Real ducts lose slight head to wall friction, causing total head to drop slightly at h6.
  • Along one streamline: Irrotational flow throughout.

Formulas Extracted from Manual

Discharge: Q = V / t

Where V = 1 L = 1 × 10⁻³ m³ is the volume collected and t is the time (t₁ = 13.87 s, t₂ = 10.82 s, t₃ = 9.83 s).

Velocity: v = Q / A

Where A is the duct cross-sectional area at that tapping.

Dynamic Head: hd = v² / (2g)

Dynamic kinetic head evaluated with standard acceleration g = 9.8 m/s².

Total Head: ht = h + hd

The sum of static pressure head in meter (h) and dynamic head (hd).

Experimental Results & Inference

Reported Result

Total head value for lower flow rate (Q₁ = 7.2 × 10⁻⁵ m³/s): 0.180 m

Bernoulli's theorem states that total head remains approximately constant along the convergent duct for a specific flow rate.

Total-Head Constancy Index (h₁–h₅)
Calculating...
Constancy = 1 − (ht,max − ht,min) ÷ ht,mean, taken over tappings h₁ to h₅ of the run in the observation table.

Inference (As stated in Laboratory Manual)

"The experiment verifies Bernoulli's theorem. As the flow passes through the venturi reduced cross-section, the velocity increases and the static pressure decreases. The total head (pressure head + dynamic head) remains approximately constant over the converging and throat section. The variation in h are due to frictional losses and experimental errors."
